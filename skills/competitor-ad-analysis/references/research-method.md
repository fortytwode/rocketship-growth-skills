# From public ads to a useful creative analysis

## Two views, two questions

Prominent ads reveal arguments worth inspecting closely. Recent launches reveal what an advertiser is putting into market now. Keep the captures and their dates separate. Neither view proves the result of the advertiser's tests.

The top ten is a practical starting set, not a magic statistical threshold. Watch the entire ads. One deeply understood example can be more useful than a hundred shallow labels. Impression ordering is more informative than longevity alone, but it is still not spend or profitability data.

## Make an evidence record before assigning a family

For each execution record the opening, actions, dialogue and overlays, persuasive mechanism, setting, emotional register, product role, payoff and call to action. Use the actual video/audio or image. Keep uncertainty visible. Attribute emotion to the ad's portrayal and evidence, not a diagnosis of the person on screen.

Mechanism, setting and emotion are separate dimensions. A public failure followed by a successful retry is a mechanism. A bakery is a setting. Disappointment or humiliation is an emotional interpretation that needs evidence. Grouping all bakery ads together would miss the underlying argument.

The deeper need may concern earning more, career progress, fitting in or recovering from a setback. Do not force these onto every ad. Show the concrete cost or consequence that supports the reading.

## Compare distinct executions

Within a candidate family, compare at least two or three distinct ads when available. Put their beats beside each other. A possible language-learning pattern is:

| Story function | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|
| Barrier | A specific communication failure |
| Concrete cost | What that failure prevents or jeopardizes |
| Intensification | Why the setback now matters more |
| Help | A credible introduction of a way forward |
| Hesitation | A reason the character might not act |
| Action | The character uses or tries the product |
| Proof | The original obstacle is tested again |
| Payoff | A changed situation, with a clear product connection |

This is an example to investigate, not a mandatory template or a claim about unseen ads. Some ads teach a phrase, stage a public test, demonstrate the product or use another structure entirely. The data should determine the families.

Look for exceptions. If one apparent family contains two different arguments, split it. If two settings share the same sequence and stakes, consider combining them. Keep the membership list and definitions consistent.

## Use counts honestly

Count selected ad IDs, reviewed ad IDs and unique media executions separately. Several IDs can use identical media. Those repeated IDs may show distribution of launches, but they are not independent examples of creative invention. A SHA-256 match proves identical bytes; a different hash does not prove a different concept. Visually review near-duplicates before drawing conclusions about idea volume.

The generated mechanic-setting and mechanic-emotion matrices count reviewed ad records. The separate execution count makes repeat usage visible. If you want an execution-weighted matrix, deduplicate by `execution_id` explicitly and label it accordingly.

## Move from structure to a new idea

Preserve the story function, then find a new situation where it matters. Do not just copy the location, actor or opening sentence. For each adaptation identify what remains, what changes, the underlying human stake and the role the product can truthfully play.

Keep an adaptation clearly labeled as a new hypothetical concept. Do not imply the reference advertiser used it, that the product supports an unverified promise, or that the ad will perform because a similar story was prominent. Creative strategy and a future test are interpretations built from evidence, not hidden access to a competitor's results.
