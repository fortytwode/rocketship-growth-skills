## Ranked decisions

1. **Select: a listening activity before a presentation.** R000005 supplies a concrete trigger and a useful boundary: the person still feels nervous. The library can demonstrate a chosen ten-minute activity without promising a change in feelings. It differs from the described evening work and can be shown with one actor and real product capture. Evidence is one synthetic review; this is a concept to test, not a validated scalable demand.
2. **Hold: train listening after a completed download.** R000002 suggests a distinct situation absent from the two descriptions, but R000003 challenges the flow it depends on. Test download discoverability, completion and subsequent offline playback first. Do not promise reliability or seamless use from these inputs.
3. **Do not select an evening variant from this evidence alone.** R000001 supports the job, but AD-02 already covers it. A new hook about the same scrolling-to-listening transition would need a distinct mechanism and a reason to test it.

Coaching and subscription objections are expectation-setting requirements for every selected concept, not permission to claim missing features or favorable commercial terms.

## Selected production brief

- **Situation:** Someone with ten minutes before a presentation repeatedly rehearses their opening line. This is a dramatized version of R000005’s reported situation; no demographic is inferred.
- **Motivation:** Choose a different activity during that interval. Keep nervousness visible or unstated; avoid a before/after emotional cure.
- **Truthful promise:** “A ten-minute guided audio session to listen to before your presentation.” This describes a supported use and duration, not a guaranteed result.
- **Opening:** The actor repeats the same first sentence twice, pauses and looks at the available time. Hook: “Another run-through of the first sentence?” This is original creative copy, not a customer quote.
- **Sequence:** Repetition → choose a recording from Evening Pocket’s library → start its real ten-minute player → listening interval → return to the presentation preparation. Do not imply a coach replied, a personalized session appeared or a brief excerpt completed the whole session.
- **Proof:** Capture the actual recording library, a session’s ten-minute duration and playback starting. Use an approved generic session; these inputs establish no presentation-specific audio. Obtain real capture before production; no offline feature is needed.
- **CTA:** “Try Evening Pocket for seven days. Subscription follows.” Use approved final trial, subscription and price terms before publishing; no “free,” savings or cancellation comparison is supplied.
- **Test question:** Does the pre-presentation premise attract useful response compared with the existing evening premise, while viewers correctly understand that this is a recording library and a trial followed by a subscription? Compare agreed campaign response measures and offer comprehension; no CPA improvement is forecast.
